What is RSS?

(If you already know RSS, here’s my feed.)

RSS is an ancient (but very useful!) internet protocol for syndicating creative work to audiences. In other words: it is a way to to easily subscribe to a website. It works like this: the site exposes a feed, which is a computer-readable overview of the site’s contents. Your newsreader periodically checks these feeds and shows updates of all the sites that you follow in reverse-chronological order.

Since the technology to make this work is called RSS, we often call these feeds RSS feeds. There’s another very similar format called Atom. Virtually all newsreaders work equally with both.

But why?

Oversimplified, an RSS feed is kinda like your Instagram feed, but rather than a computer algorithm feeding you an endless stream of mildly entertaining mediocracy, you choose whom to follow yourself. That means you are in control over your media consumption. There will be no ads and no doom-scrolling reels at 3am. It’s a bit like a newsletter, but in a nice reading environment, instead of… y’know, email (ugh).

But! if you email is (somehow??) your thing, you could also configure your RSS reader to deliver new posts to your inbox of course!

Cool, how do I get started?

To subscribe to an RSS feed, you use a newsreader app. There’s lots of choices.

RSS is a protocol, and is thus naturally free to use (like how language is free to use), but some apps provide additional features in a paid subscription. Most of these apps also allow you to sync your subscriptions between devices, but limit the amount of subscriptions you can have.

  • Inoreader. Free up to 150 subscriptions.
    Offers iOS, iPadOS, and Android apps, and a web app.

  • NewsBlur. Free up to 64 subscriptions.
    Offers iOS, iPadOS and Android apps, and a web app.

  • NetNewsWire. Offers iOS, iPadOS and MacOS apps. The apps themselves are entirely free and don’t put limits on the amount of feeds. However, if you use an online service like Inoreader or NewsBlur to sync your subscriptions, their limits still apply.

  • The Old Reader. Free up to 100 subscriptions.
    Offers iOS and Android apps, and a web app. Contains ads.

But how do I use RSS?

Most sites that offer an RSS will probably have a link saying “RSS/Atom” or an orage icon similar to this: somewhere on the page. If they do, you can subscribe to them.

Subscribing is simple: you subscribe by entering the URL to their blog, website, channel, profile, whatever into your RSS reader app.

Then, the app will periodically check the feed for new posts. When you feel like it, you open up the app, and they will be waiting for you in good old reverse-chronological order. Some apps also provide other filters for showing the content, but it’s always under your control.

Where can I find feeds to subscribe to?

The best way to discover blogs to follow is by simply surfing the web. Not with a search engine, but by following links from site to site. There’s tons of interesting stuff out there. Often, interesting blogs have a blogroll: a list of other blogs that they like.

Here are some starting points:

  • My blogroll
  • ooh.directory
  • blogroll.org

In addition to most blogs, lots of other sites also offer RSS feeds. For example, every YouTube channel has an RSS feed (try it: simply enter the channel URL in your RSS reader!). And most news sites (like NOS) offer RSS feeds too, as do weather forecasts.

And RSS is also the protocol underpinning virtually all modern podcasting apps, but that is a story for another day.
